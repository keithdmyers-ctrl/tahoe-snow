What does the score mean?
The score (0-100) answers "should I drive to Tahoe?" It weighs snowfall (35%), snow quality (20%), lift availability (15%), avalanche danger (15%), chain controls (10%), and model agreement (5%), with a weekend/holiday crowd penalty. 75+ = strong go, 45-74 = marginal, below 45 = save your gas.
Why does this show different snow amounts than my iPhone?
iPhone Weather uses a single model (usually Apple's WeatherKit). This system runs 4 models (GFS, ECMWF, ICON, HRRR) side by side and shows you when they agree and when they don't. It also applies snow physics — the same 10mm of liquid precipitation produces 8" of powder in a cold storm vs. 2" of cement in a warm storm. Phone apps skip this step.
What do the confidence ratings mean?
High: All models agree within 2" — you can plan around this number. Medium: Models spread up to 5" apart — the trend is right but the exact amount is uncertain. Low: Models disagree by 5"+ — could go either way, check back tomorrow.
What's the difference between base, mid, and peak?
Temperature drops ~3.5°F per 1,000 feet of elevation. At Heavenly, the base (6,540') might be getting rain while the peak (10,067') gets a foot of powder. Each zone gets its own temperature, wind, precipitation type, and snow quality forecast.
What is SLR?
Snow-to-Liquid Ratio. It's how fluffy or dense the snow is. SLR 20:1 means 20 inches of snow per 1 inch of water — cold, dry blower powder. SLR 5:1 means 5 inches per 1 inch of water — heavy Sierra cement. Temperature is the main driver.
How often does the data update?
The web dashboard caches data for 15 minutes. Weather models update every 1-12 hours depending on the model. SNOTEL stations report daily. Lift status and chain controls update every few minutes. Use the Refresh button to force a fresh fetch.
What is SNOTEL?
USDA automated snow monitoring stations. They measure snow depth (ultrasonic sensor), snow water equivalent (pressure pillow), and temperature at remote mountain locations. This system pulls from 10 stations across the Tahoe basin.
What does the AQI number mean?
Air Quality Index (EPA standard). 0-50 Good (green), 51-100 Moderate (yellow), 101-150 Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups (orange), 151-200 Unhealthy (red), 201+ Very Unhealthy/Hazardous (purple). Important for outdoor activities especially during fire season.

Snow Physics

Snow-to-Liquid Ratio (Roebber 2003): Temperature determines crystal structure. Cold storms (-18°C) produce fluffy 20:1 powder. Warm storms (0°C) produce dense 5:1 Sierra cement. Wind speed (Judson & Doesken 2000) and humidity further adjust the ratio.

Orographic Enhancement: Mountains force air upward, squeezing out more precipitation on windward slopes. A peak in strong WSW flow can get ~2x the precipitation of the base. Based on wind direction alignment, elevation gain, and wind speed, with CAPE coupling (Kirshbaum & Smith 2008).

Precipitation Type (Stull 2011): Uses wet-bulb temperature (not dry-bulb) to classify Snow/Mix/Rain. This matches NWS operational methodology. A logistic probability function provides smooth transitions instead of hard cutoffs.

Snow Settling (Kojima 1967): Fresh snow compresses 15-40% in the first 24 hours depending on temperature and wind. Warmer = faster settling.

Lake Effect: Lake Tahoe's relatively warm surface (4.5°C in winter) enhances snowfall on the east shore during NW-N wind events when the lake-air temperature difference exceeds 10°C.

Elevation Adjustment

Temperature drops ~5.5-6.5°C per km of elevation gain. The lapse rate is computed from three sources in priority order:

  1. Reno radiosonde (twice daily) — actual measured atmosphere profile
  2. SNOTEL surface regression — real-time linear fit across 10 stations at different elevations
  3. Standard lapse rate — 5.5°C/km (moist) or 6.5°C/km (dry)

Terrain-adjusted temperature also accounts for slope aspect: south-facing slopes warm more during the day, north-facing slopes preserve snow longer.

Oakland Rain Prediction

Seven sources fused with weighted averaging: NBM (35%), NWS POP (30%), Open-Meteo model agreement (20%), Zambretti barometer (15%), plus dewpoint and PWS ground truth modifiers. The Zambretti Forecaster (1920, Negretti & Zambra) uses local barometric pressure trends to detect approaching weather systems 2-6 hours before models update.

Forecast Verification

Every day, forecasts are compared to observations. Per-model MAE (Mean Absolute Error), RMSE, and bias are tracked. After 14+ days, bias corrections are applied automatically. Model weights adapt with a 14-day exponential half-life — models that performed well recently get more influence.

Strengths

  • Multi-model blending with honest uncertainty (shows when models disagree)
  • Real snow physics (SLR, orographic, wet-bulb) instead of raw model output
  • Per-elevation forecasts (base/mid/peak) for each resort
  • Self-correcting: verification loop adapts model weights over time
  • 14+ independent data sources cross-referenced

Limitations

  • All models share similar biases for the Sierra (e.g., underpredicting upslope enhancement)
  • No radar integration — storm timing relies on model forecasts, not real-time observation
  • Orographic multiplier is simplified (no Froude number, no barrier-relative flow)
  • Forecast verification needs a full season (~50+ storms) to be statistically meaningful
  • Snow settling model uses simplified exponential decay, not full metamorphism physics
  • Local sensors required for Oakland barometric prediction (system degrades gracefully without them)
